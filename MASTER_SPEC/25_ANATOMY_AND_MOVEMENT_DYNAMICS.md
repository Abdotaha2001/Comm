# PART 25 — ANATOMY & MOVEMENT DYNAMICS (BIOMECHANICS / KINESIOLOGY)

> Read `00_INDEX.md` first. The anatomical & biomechanical ground truth for the **biomechanics model (M27)**, **stroke-quality scorer (M25)**, and **injury-risk model (M29)** — and the math in Part 19 §C7.

## A. Muscles & joints by stroke (what fires, where)
| Action | Primary muscles | Joint actions |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Forehand drive/loop** | biceps brachii, deltoid (+ pecs, lats) | **internal arm rotation + shoulder adduction**; elbow extension |
| **Backhand drive/loop** | wrist flexors, finger extensors, deltoid | **arm abduction + shoulder rotation**; wrist snap |
| **Trunk drive (both)** | hip rotators, obliques, erector spinae | **axial trunk rotation** (transverse plane) |
| **Lower body / power** | gluteus, quadriceps, hamstrings, gastrocnemius | hip & knee flexion/extension; **ankle absorbs ground force** |
| Grip/touch | forearm flexors/extensors, intrinsic hand | wrist/finger control |

> Racket velocity correlates with **shoulder internal-rotation + adduction angular velocity** (FH) and **arm abduction + shoulder rotation** (BH).

## B. The kinetic chain (ground → ball)
```
Ground reaction → Ankle → Knee → Hip / Trunk (axial rotation) → Shoulder → Elbow → Wrist → Ball
```
- **> 50% of total force** is generated by the **lower limbs + hip + torso**; the upper limb mostly **transmits & directs** it.
- **Link strengths:** trunk = *very strong*, shoulder = *strong*, elbow = *medium*, **wrist = weak link but highest velocity** (it converts the chain's energy into ball spin/speed → also the highest injury exposure).
- **Sequencing = proximal → distal** with correct timing: ankle/hip fire first, wrist last & fastest. Efficiency = tight, ordered velocity cascade (Part 19 §C7).

## C. Joint ROM & velocities (measurable targets for M25/M27)
- **Shoulder angular displacement > 120°** during the FH acceleration phase.
- **Faster elbow-extension speed** and **greater shoulder ROM** distinguish elite from beginner.
- Ankle "rapid reaction" (stretch-shortening) governs energy-transfer efficiency up the chain.

## D. Planes of motion
- **Transverse (horizontal):** dominant — trunk axial rotation drives the stroke.
- **Sagittal:** forward weight transfer, knee/elbow flexion-extension.
- **Frontal:** lateral footwork, shoulder ab/adduction.

## E. Skill-level signature (what "good" looks like)
- **Elite:** full-body kinetic chain; uses **shoulder + wrist joint power**; legs/core lead; large synchronized ROM.
- **Beginner:** **forearm/wrist dominant**, little leg/trunk contribution → less power, more injury risk, less spin.
- This contrast **is** the stroke-quality rubric for M25.

## F. Injury hotspots (feeds M29 injury-risk)
- **Wrist:** highest velocity/repetition → overuse.
- **Shoulder:** rotation load (rotator cuff).
- **Lower back:** repetitive axial rotation.
- **Knee/ankle:** rapid change-of-direction loading.
> Risk rises when the chain is **broken** (e.g. all-arm strokes) or with **asymmetry** (Part 06 M30) — the model flags these.

## G. Platform implications
| Need | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| Biomechanics model (M27) | 3D pose → joint angles/velocities → **kinetic-chain sequencing** score (Part 19 §C7) |
| Stroke quality (M25) | compare athlete's chain/ROM to the elite signature in §E |
| Injury risk (M29) | load on weak links (wrist/shoulder/back) + chain-break + asymmetry |
| Para variants (Part 24) | wheelchair = seated chain (trunk+upper limb, no leg drive) → **different reference model** |
| Coaching (Part 08/22) | corrective cues target the broken link ("drive from legs/hips, not the arm") |
