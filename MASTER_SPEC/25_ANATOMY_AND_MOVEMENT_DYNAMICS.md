# PART 25 — ANATOMY & MOVEMENT DYNAMICS (BIOMECHANICS / KINESIOLOGY)

> Read `00_INDEX.md` first. The anatomical & biomechanical ground truth for the **biomechanics model (M27)**, **stroke-quality scorer (M25)**, and **injury-risk model (M29)** — and the math in Part 19 §C7.

## A. Muscles & joints by stroke (what fires, where)
| Action | Primary muscles | Joint actions |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Forehand drive/loop** | biceps brachii, deltoid (+ pecs, lats) | **internal arm rotation + shoulder adduction**; elbow extension |
| **Backhand drive/loop** | wrist flexors, finger extensors, deltoid | **arm abduction + shoulder rotation**; wrist snap |
| **Trunk drive (both)** | hip rotators, obliques, erector spinae | **axial trunk rotation** (transverse plane) |
| **Lower body / power** | gluteus, quadriceps, hamstrings, gastrocnemius | hip & knee flexion/extension; **ankle absorbs ground force** |
| Grip/touch | forearm flexors/extensors, intrinsic hand | wrist/finger control |

> Racket velocity correlates with **shoulder internal-rotation + adduction angular velocity** (FH) and **arm abduction + shoulder rotation** (BH).

## B. The kinetic chain (ground → ball)
```
Ground reaction → Ankle → Knee → Hip / Trunk (axial rotation) → Shoulder → Elbow → Wrist → Ball
```
- **> 50% of total force** is generated by the **lower limbs + hip + torso**; the upper limb mostly **transmits & directs** it.
- **Link strengths:** trunk = *very strong*, shoulder = *strong*, elbow = *medium*, **wrist = weak link but highest velocity** (it converts the chain's energy into ball spin/speed → also the highest injury exposure).
- **Sequencing = proximal → distal** with correct timing: ankle/hip fire first, wrist last & fastest. Efficiency = tight, ordered velocity cascade (Part 19 §C7).

## C. Joint ROM & velocities (measurable targets for M25/M27)
- **Shoulder angular displacement > 120°** during the FH acceleration phase.
- **Faster elbow-extension speed** and **greater shoulder ROM** distinguish elite from beginner.
- Ankle "rapid reaction" (stretch-shortening) governs energy-transfer efficiency up the chain.

## D. Planes of motion
- **Transverse (horizontal):** dominant — trunk axial rotation drives the stroke.
- **Sagittal:** forward weight transfer, knee/elbow flexion-extension.
- **Frontal:** lateral footwork, shoulder ab/adduction.

## E. Skill-level signature (what "good" looks like)
- **Elite:** full-body kinetic chain; uses **shoulder + wrist joint power**; legs/core lead; large synchronized ROM.
- **Beginner:** **forearm/wrist dominant**, little leg/trunk contribution → less power, more injury risk, less spin.
- This contrast **is** the stroke-quality rubric for M25.

## F. Injury hotspots (feeds M29 injury-risk)
- **Wrist:** highest velocity/repetition → overuse.
- **Shoulder:** rotation load (rotator cuff).
- **Lower back:** repetitive axial rotation.
- **Knee/ankle:** rapid change-of-direction loading.
> Risk rises when the chain is **broken** (e.g. all-arm strokes) or with **asymmetry** (Part 06 M30) — the model flags these.

## G. Platform implications
| Need | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| Biomechanics model (M27) | 3D pose → joint angles/velocities → **kinetic-chain sequencing** score (Part 19 §C7) |

## H. Serve biomechanics (the most kinematically complex action)
- **Phases:** *preparation* (start → **ball-toss release, BTR**) → *propulsion-acceleration* (BTR → impact). The **toss initiates the kinetic chain**; uniquely, the player may elect **not** to strike.
- **Key joints:** shoulder rotation · elbow flexion-extension · **wrist flexion-extension** · hand **supination**. Higher **wrist angular velocity → higher ball & racket speed** (both short and long serves).
- **Chain:** forceful **leg drive** + trunk rotation (frontal & sagittal) transfer angular momentum legs→trunk→arm; the **lumbar spine counter-rotates** (hyperextension→flexion, right→left twist) just before impact.
- **Elite signature:** larger max **shoulder internal-rotation, elbow varus, wrist radial-deviation torques** + higher shoulder joint-power. Short vs long serves differ mainly in wrist/elbow/supination kinematics → a discriminative feature for the serve classifier (M22).

## I. Footwork kinetics — cross-step vs chassé (measured)
| GRF component (normalized) | Cross-step | Chassé |
|---------------------------|-----------|--------|
| Posterior (drive) | **0.89** | 0.82 |
| Lateral | -0.38 | **-0.46** |
| Vertical | 1.73 | **1.90** |
- **Two GRF peaks** per stroke: landing peak + **kicking (push-off) peak**.
- Chassé loads **medial-lateral** more (side prep for the next stroke); cross-step generates more **posterior drive** for covering distance. → distinct kinetic signatures for the footwork model (M26) and for **load/asymmetry** (M29/M30).

## J. Deeper anatomy & neuromuscular detail
- **Shoulder:** rotator cuff (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis) + deltoid + scapular stabilizers (serratus, trapezius).
- **Forearm/wrist:** flexor carpi radialis/ulnaris, extensor carpi group, pronator/supinator (spin & snap).
- **Core chains:** anterior (rectus abdominis, obliques) + posterior (erector spinae, multifidus) → the axial-rotation engine.
- **EMG timing:** activation follows **proximal→distal** (legs/hip → trunk → shoulder → forearm/wrist) — mistimed firing = energy leak (the M27 sequencing check).
- **Stretch-shortening cycle (SSC):** pre-stretch (eccentric) then explosive concentric in legs/trunk stores & releases elastic energy → power; ankle SSC governs transfer efficiency.
- **Anthropometrics / COM:** segment masses via **Dempster ratios** → center-of-mass & balance (base-of-support projection), feeding M27 balance metrics.
- **Neuromuscular:** reaction time, proprioception, and fast-twitch recruitment underpin the <4 s explosive actions (Part 23).

## K. Injury mechanisms & prevention (prehab) — feeds M29
| Site | Mechanism | Prehab |
|------|-----------|--------|
| **Wrist** | rapid repetitive flexion/snap, overuse | grip technique, load management, forearm strengthening |
| **Shoulder** | rotator-cuff strain from explosive rotation, poor warm-up | band pull-aparts, shoulder dislocates, scapular wall-slides, face pulls, external rotations |
| **Lower back** | **asymmetric** repetitive trunk rotation (one direction) → lumbar load | core stability, anti-rotation work, mobility, contralateral balancing |
| **Knee** | patellar tendinitis / meniscus from lateral cuts & squats | single-leg lunges, eccentric quad work, landing mechanics |
| **Ankle** | sprains from rapid direction change | balance/proprioception, single-leg stability |
> Three pillars: **warm-up + targeted strengthening + suitable equipment.** The asymmetry of the sport (§F) is the root of back/shoulder issues → the model flags one-directional overload.

## L. Grip-specific wrist mechanics
- **Shakehand:** neutral wrist, balanced FH/BH; transition point at the crossover.
- **Penhold:** greater wrist freedom/supination on FH (more serve & FH spin), historically weaker BH → solved by **RPB** (reverse-penhold-backhand) using the racket's back. → the biomech model must condition on grip (Part 04 M19) when scoring wrist action.
