# PART 23 — ATHLETE DEVELOPMENT SCIENCE: LTAD · PHYSICAL · MENTAL

## A. Long-Term Athlete Development (LTAD — Balyi's 7 stages)
It takes **8–12 years** to reach elite. Progression is by **maturational level, not chronological age** (identify early / average / late maturers; males & females differ).

| # | Stage | Focus |
|---|-------|-------|
| 1 | **Active Start** | basic movement, play |
| 2 | **FUNdamentals** (~until age 6) | fundamental movement skills, fun, varied movement |
| 3 | **Learn to Train** | general sports skills + basic TT technique |
| 4 | **Train to Train** | build engine + technique consistency; the big skill window |
| 5 | **Train to Compete** | event-specific, tactics, match volume |
| 6 | **Train to Win** | peak performance, individualized, periodized |
| 7 | **Active for Life** | lifelong participation / transition out |

### Windows of trainability — the **5 S's**
Each capacity is trainable for life, but has a **critical window of accelerated adaptation**:
**Stamina · Strength · Speed · Skill · Suppleness.** → The plan must train **the right S at the right stage** (e.g. emphasize **skill & speed** in pre-/early-puberty windows; **strength** around/after peak height velocity). Doing the wrong S at the wrong time wastes the window.

## B. Physical conditioning for table tennis
### The sport's physical demands (design targets)
- Ball up to **120 km/h** (peaks ~**160**); **30+ strokes/min**; each action **< 4 s**; rest **~15 s**.
- **Energy systems:** **~96.5% oxidative (aerobic)**, **~2.5% phosphagen (ATP-PC)**, **~1% glycolytic**. → Train a strong **aerobic base** + **repeat-sprint / ATP-PC** ability for explosive 4-second bursts. (Long glycolytic conditioning is largely irrelevant — a common training error.)

### Key physical attributes
Agility · leg strength & explosive power · change-of-direction · speed · grip strength · reaction time · **core stability** (postural control + efficient force transfer).
- **Periodized core training** → improves muscular endurance, agility, balance.
- **Legs/lower body** → weight transfer + power + stability in attack and defense.
- Reaction time is partly trained by the rally itself + targeted drills (Part 22).

## C. Mental / psychological training (the mental standards)
**Psychological Skills Training (PST) toolkit:** self-talk · **imagery/visualization** · **goal setting** · mindfulness · relaxation/breathing · **pre-performance routines (PPR)**.
- **Pressure management:** set **process goals** (not outcome/score); controlled **breathing** to calm nerves; focus on the **present & the ball, not the score**.
- **Imagery:** 1st-person (timing, open skills) vs 3rd-person (form, positioning).
- **PPR:** consistent warm-up + visualization + calming → signals the mind to focus; its **consistency is measurable** (links Part 14 emotion/routine recognition, Part 07 clutch index).

## D. Platform implications
| Need | Mechanism |
|------|-----------|
| Talent framework (Part 08) | fills the **physical / mental** standards & promotion criteria per stage |
| Junior profile (Part 17) | store **maturation status** (early/avg/late), LTAD stage, active trainability windows |
| Plan generator (Part 08) | **stage- & window-aware** prescription (right S at right time); avoid maturation-mismatch injury (Part 06) |
| Conditioning (Part 06) | energy-system-specific (aerobic base + ATP-PC), core & legs, agility/reaction |
| Mental metrics | PPR consistency, pressure/clutch behavior (Parts 07/14) → mental-readiness index |
| Talent ID | maturation-adjusted assessment (don't over-credit early maturers) |

> **Key safeguard:** because progression is maturation-based, the platform must **never** judge a 12-year-old by chronological-age norms alone — it adjusts for early/average/late maturation, protecting late maturers from being wrongly cut and early maturers from being over-trained.
